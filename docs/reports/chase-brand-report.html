Prepared for SOCRadar as part of a Senior CTI Ops Engineer candidate project. Not an official SOCRadar publication. Indicators are defanged.

Executive summary

In the 7 days to 15 November 2026 we tracked 47 phishing pages impersonating Chase, 3% of all phishing pages tracked in that period; 39 were live when checked. Hostnames and paths most often add the words app, online, bank, mobile. Most pages are hosted in United States, and the most used top-level domain is .com.

The most significant campaign: an operator is running a Chase phishing campaign across 1 host on 1 hosting address, collecting usernames / email addresses. The hosts share infrastructure and page content, so we assess them as one operation. 1 page was live when last checked.

We recommend blocking the listed hosts and addresses at web and mail gateways, filing abuse reports with the hosting providers, and hunting for the patterns described below. Details and indicators follow.

How the kit works

The pages imitate Chase. Analysis of the page code shows:

  • The page asks for usernames / email addresses.
  • Collected data is sent to an attacker mailbox, form_service, an off-site form endpoint.

Figure 8. A page from this campaign titled “Jian Chang Chambers | Property, Probate & Debt Recovery Lawy”, imitating Chase and asking for usernames / email addresses.

Impact

  • Customers: a victim who completes the flow loses usernames / email addresses, which allows follow-up phishing.
  • Chase: fraud losses and customer reimbursement, plus support load from compromised accounts.
