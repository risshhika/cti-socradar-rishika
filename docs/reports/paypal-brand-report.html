PayPal phishing: last 7 days

Brand activity in the last 7 days and the leading campaign: PayPal phishing campaign on one dedicated server

Reporter: Rishika Desai   |   Date: 15 November 2026   |   Classification: TLP:CLEAR   |   Report ID: CTI-P4-2026-11-PAYPAL-040

Prepared for SOCRadar as part of a Senior CTI Ops Engineer candidate project. Not an official SOCRadar publication. Indicators are defanged.

Executive summary

In the 7 days to 15 November 2026 we tracked 37 phishing pages impersonating PayPal, 2% of all phishing pages tracked in that period; 30 were live when checked. Hostnames and paths most often add the words service, app, id, auth. Most pages are hosted in United States, and the most used top-level domain is .dev.

The most significant campaign: a single operator is running a PayPal phishing campaign across 4 hosts on one dedicated server, collecting account passwords, one-time / MFA codes, usernames / email addresses. The hosts share infrastructure and page content, so we assess them as one operation. 4 pages were live when last checked.

We recommend blocking the listed hosts and addresses at web and mail gateways, filing abuse reports with the hosting providers, and hunting for the patterns described below. Details and indicators follow.

How the kit works

The pages imitate PayPal. Analysis of the page code shows:

  • The page asks for account passwords, one-time / MFA codes, usernames / email addresses.
  • The page template is not a publicly documented kit and is tracked as a new kit.

Related activity, not linked

Other PayPal pages share the same kit or title but use different hosting, so we track them separately.

  • shopfc24h[.]com (first seen 10 Nov 2026, 00:16 UTC). Same page title on different hosting, so the link is not established yet.

Impact

  • Customers: a victim who completes the flow loses account passwords, one-time / MFA codes, usernames / email addresses, which allows account takeover and real-time bypass of one-time codes and follow-up phishing.
  • PayPal: fraud losses and customer reimbursement, plus support load from compromised accounts.

Recommendations

For the brand-protection team

  • report 150.241.66[.]223 to the abuse desk of KYONIX Kyonix Networks Limited.
  • Watch for new hosts with the hunting queries above, checked at least daily.
  • Move customers who use SMS codes toward app-based or passkey sign-in, and tell them the bank never asks for a code on a page reached from a link.

For SOC and fraud teams

  • Block 150.241.66[.]223 and the hosts listed above at web and mail gateways.
  • Flag PayPal sessions that start from a new device right after a sign-in from an unfamiliar location, for fraud review.
