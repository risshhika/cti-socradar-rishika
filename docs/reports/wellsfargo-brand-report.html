Executive summary

In the 7 days to 15 November 2026 we tracked 27 phishing pages impersonating Wells Fargo, 2% of all phishing pages tracked in that period; 25 were live when checked. Hostnames and paths most often add the words bank, help, mobile, online. Most pages are hosted in Australia, and the most used top-level domain is .com.

The most significant campaign: an operator is running a Wells Fargo phishing campaign across 1 host on workers.dev hosts, collecting account passwords, usernames / email addresses. The hosts share page content but no infrastructure, so the operator link is weak. 1 page was live when last checked.

We recommend blocking the listed hosts and addresses at web and mail gateways, filing abuse reports with the hosting providers, and hunting for the patterns described below. Details and indicators follow.

How the kit works

The pages imitate Wells Fargo. Analysis of the page code shows:

  • The page asks for account passwords, usernames / email addresses.
  • To avoid automated analysis, the page checks the visitor's browser before showing the form.

Impact

  • Customers: a victim who completes the flow loses account passwords, usernames / email addresses, which allows account takeover and follow-up phishing.
  • Wells Fargo: fraud losses and customer reimbursement, plus support load from compromised accounts.

Recommendations

For the bank and its brand-protection team

  • Report the hosts to workers.dev for takedown.
  • Watch for new hosts with the hunting queries above, checked at least daily.
  • Encourage customers to turn on app-based or passkey sign-in, so a stolen password alone does not open the account.

For SOC and fraud teams

  • Block the hosts listed above at web and mail gateways.
  • Flag Wells Fargo sessions that start from a new device right after a sign-in from an unfamiliar location, for fraud review.
